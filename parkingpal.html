Parking Pal: first prototype

Product development engineer, student startup

The Problem

Finding parking in Los Angeles is a daily struggle. In busy areas like Downtown or Santa Monica, I often spend 15-30 minutes circling for a spot, dealing with unclear signs, inconsistent meter rules, and the risk of receiving tickets or having my vehicle towed. Not only does it waste time and fuel, but it also contributes to traffic and pollution. So I wanted to build something to help other people with the same struggle.

Contribution

Researched and built a proof of concept using microcontrollers and magnetic sensors, with SolidWorks for the mechanical design. Real-time sensor data went to a mobile app built with JavaScript, HTML/CSS, and C++, with a Node.js server linking the hardware and software.

The Result

Successfully developed a functional MVP of a sensor system for the Entrepreneurship Society Incubator Showcase. The prototype demonstrated real-time magnetic field detection with consistent accuracy and stability in controlled environments. Key outcomes included reliable signal acquisition, integration with a prototype, and initial data visualization capabilities. The MVP served as a proof of concept for scalable magnetic sensing applications, attracting interest from potential collaborators and stakeholders during the showcase.

We also impressed the whole room with the demo, except for the three engineering students in the booth next to us.

What I would do differently

I would not use a reed switch again: testing showed its detection range was too short. I also considered a solenoid magnetometer, but it was not cost-efficient, so I dropped that idea.
