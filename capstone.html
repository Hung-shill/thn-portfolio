Autonomous Mobility Assistive Robot

Senior capstone, LMU, Feb 2026 to now

The Problem

Caregivers in elder care facilities and at home cannot watch every fall-risk resident around the clock, and the risk is highest at night. Commercial elder-care robots exist, but they cost tens of thousands of dollars.

What we are building

A robot that gives physical support rather than companionship. It runs scheduled patrol rounds overnight so falls get reported and answered faster, and it escorts residents on nighttime walks with a handle they can lean on, similar to a smart walker.

Design research with caregivers set one firm rule: the robot must be easy to see and hear, because a silent moving robot is itself a trip hazard for someone with poor vision.

What I did

  • Ran an FMEA (IEC 60812) on the robot's power and electrical system, flagging 24 failure modes and 14 for corrective action.
  • Compiled a 48-line BOM and a $5,000 budget across 5 subsystems, cutting power system cost by about $883 to fit the budget.
  • Drafted 21 specs and scored 27 concepts in weighted decision matrices, which surfaced a battery pack with a 20% run-time margin.

Status

In progress. Photos and build updates will be added as the robot comes together.
